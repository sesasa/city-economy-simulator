How close to history?

Model: citysim Stage 11, a stock-flow consistent town (one tick = a quarter, every dollar and unit of output booked in a double-entry ledger and checked every tick). Money is in the model's dollars (a town of 10,000; $1 in the model ≈ $1,000). The central bank follows a rule (inflation and jobs, with extra cuts when shares slide and jobs are being lost); only four moments are scripted from history: the 1996 warning, the 1998 rescue cuts, the Y2K liquidity, and the 2003 promise to hold rates at 1%. Everything else — the bubble, the crash, the fibre glut, the bankruptcies and the winners — comes out of the mechanisms. Known gaps: the slump arrives about two years late, the 2001 rate cuts are too slow, and the fibre build-out is magnified about six times. Company names are fictional.
